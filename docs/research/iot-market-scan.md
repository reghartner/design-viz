## 2. Distinctive features and architectures

- **Ultra-low-power battery designs (Blink).** Blink's two-year battery life on two AA lithium
  cells comes from proprietary chip technology (sourced); the widely described mechanism is a
  low-power always-on path (PIR + efficient wake logic) that keeps the main video SoC asleep
  until an event, an architecture other battery-cam vendors approximate with PIR wake
  (inferred). Lithium chemistry is required — alkaline cells cut life to months.
- **Radar / mmWave sensing (Ring, Ecobee, Aqara).** Ring's radar measures distance and angle
  of moving objects, enabling 3D Motion Detection thresholds by real distance and "Bird's Eye
  View" — an aerial map of the path a visitor took, sent with the notification. Ecobee's
  doorbell uses radar for approach accuracy. Aqara's G410 doorbell uses mmWave to sense
  approach before the camera wakes (mechanism inferred), and its FP2 sensor uses mmWave for
  multi-zone room presence, fall detection, and sleep monitoring — position tracking, not just
  motion.
- **On-device vs hub vs cloud AI — three camps.** (1) Camera-edge: UniFi G6 cameras carry a
  multi-TOPS engine for person/vehicle/animal/package; Tapo D260 and Aqara do face recognition
  on-device. (2) Hub-edge: eufy's HomeBase S380 runs "BionicMind" facial recognition locally
  across all paired cameras; Ubiquiti's AI Key is a dedicated appliance that re-processes
  camera detections with bigger models (rated 1,000 detections/hour); Tapo's H500 hub adds AI
  to dumber cameras. (3) Cloud LLM: Google's Gemini for Home generates descriptive
  notifications ("dog jumps out of playpen") and natural-language history search; Ring's AI
  tier does AI Video Descriptions and AI Video Search in the cloud.
- **Pre-roll ring buffers (Ring; Nest analog).** Ring Pre-Roll shows up to 6 extra seconds
  before the trigger — battery models capture a color low-res rolling buffer, wired "Advanced
  Pre-Roll" keeps 1080p with audio; this implies a continuously-written circular buffer in RAM
  that is prepended when the PIR/radar fires (mechanism inferred). Nest's battery devices keep
  up to one hour of events in local flash when offline and upload when connectivity returns —
  a store-and-forward buffer rather than a pre-roll.
- **HomeKit Secure Video (Apple as the vendor cloud).** Eve, Logitech, Aqara, eufy (S330),
  VOCOlinc: the camera streams to the user's Apple TV/HomePod, which runs person/vehicle/
  animal/package detection locally, encrypts end-to-end, and stores in iCloud where Apple
  cannot view it; camera counts are gated by iCloud+ tier (200 GB = 1 camera, 2 TB = 5, 6 TB
  = unlimited).
- **Matter 1.5 cameras (Nov 2025 spec, first devices early 2026).** Matter now standardizes
  cameras: WebRTC live streaming, multi-stream, PTZ control, detection and privacy zones, and
  continuous or event recording, over Wi-Fi/Ethernet/PoE. Aqara, Eve, and Xthings ship Matter
  cameras in early 2026; TP-Link tested camera models with the CSA; SmartThings announced
  controller-side support; Matter 1.5.1 refined camera behavior.
- **Cellular cameras and cellular backup.** Reolink Go PT Ultra and Altas Go PT, eufy 4G LTE
  Cam S330, and Arlo Go 2 run on 4G SIMs for off-grid sites; Arlo Go 2 fails over between LTE
  and Wi-Fi. eufy and Reolink record locally with no storage fee, Arlo/Vosker lean on cloud.
  Separately, SimpliSafe's Base Station uses built-in cellular as its monitoring path with a
  24-hour battery, so alarm events reach the monitoring center through ISP and power outages.
- **Local hub recording.** eufy HomeBase S380 (up to 16 TB, 16 cams + 34 sensors), Blink Sync
  Module 2 (10 cams; USB storage — inferred) and Sync Module XR (extends radio range to
  distant cameras), Reolink Home Hub family (encrypted microSD), Ubiquiti UNVR G2 Pro (HDD
  array for the fleet), Lorex Fusion NVR/DVRs (8-32 channels), Tapo H500 (expandable storage
  + chime). Architectural common thread: cameras associate with the hub, video and AI stay on
  the LAN, and the vendor cloud handles only signaling/remote access (inferred).
- **Continuous recording on battery (Reolink Altas).** 20,000 mAh batteries plus solar top-up
  and low-power encoding let Altas cameras record 24/7 without wiring — normally exclusive to
  wired cameras; "ColorX" sensors give color night vision without a spotlight.
- **End-to-end encryption tiers (Ring).** Ring's opt-in E2EE locks video with a passphrase
  key held only on enrolled mobile devices — Ring/Amazon and police cannot decrypt — at the
  cost of Echo/Alexa viewing and (inferred) cloud AI features that need plaintext video; Ring
  shipped a further encryption update in August 2026.
- **Human-in-the-loop deterrence.** SimpliSafe Active Guard: camera AI flags a person, a live
  agent watches and talks/sirens/lights them away (overnight or 24/7 by plan). Ring's Virtual
  Security Guard tier is the same pattern; Arlo Secure Plus includes emergency response
  features.
- **Cross-camera tracking (eufy) and networked search (Ring).** eufy's 2026 AI hands an event
  from camera to camera as the subject moves, then delivers one auto-edited clip of the whole
  path — implying shared subject re-identification across devices on the HomeBase (inferred).
  Ring's "Search Party" applies the network idea across homes to find lost pets.
- **Subscription-gating as architecture.** Arlo removed free cloud storage on new cameras;
  Ring gates Familiar Faces/AI Search behind its AI tier; Google gates 24/7 history (wired
  cams only, 10 days) and Gemini features behind Home Premium; Wyze free tier caps clips at
  12 seconds. Counter-positioned vendors (Tapo, Netatmo, Lorex, Reolink, Ubiquiti, eufy) sell
  "no monthly fee" as the feature, enabled by local storage/AI.
- **Physical privacy mechanisms.** Wyze's 2026 Indoor Cam Pan rotates its lens into the body;
  Tapo C225 has a privacy shield; HKSV and Matter support software privacy zones; detection
  zones are universal (inferred for the last).
- **Cameras as smart-home hubs (Aqara).** G5 Pro and G410 embed a Zigbee hub, Matter
  Controller, and Thread Border Router in the camera, and run automations locally during
  internet outages — the camera is the home's control plane, not just a sensor.
- **Doorbells engineered around power (Ecobee vs Ring).** Ecobee ships wired-only with no
  battery specifically for extreme-temperature reliability; Ring's 2026 flagship went the
  other way — first 4K battery doorbell with a quick-release pack and solar charger options.

## 3. Use cases not obvious from spec sheets

- **Geofenced arming.** Arlo switches Home/Away modes from phone GPS on a first-in/last-out
  basis (armed only when the last phone leaves the radius); it silently fails if the app is
  logged out or location permission is off — users pair it with schedules as a fallback.
  Comparable mode systems exist on Ring and others (inferred).
- **Activity zones and detection filtering.** Users draw zones to ignore streets/sidewalks;
  Ring's radar adds real distance thresholds so alerts fire only within N feet. Matter 1.5
  standardizes detection zones cross-platform.
- **Doorbell answering without answering.** Ring's Alexa+ Greetings lets the assistant greet
  visitors, screen them, and take delivery instructions autonomously; quick-reply canned
  messages are the manual version (latter inferred as widely shipped).
- **Neighbor sharing and its politics.** Ring Neighbors (10M+ users) lets households share
  clips; voluntary sharing with law enforcement continues via the app, and the platform has
  drawn privacy criticism (GPS coordinate leakage report). E2EE-minded buyers actively avoid
  this category of feature.
- **Emergency dispatch integration.** SimpliSafe's monitored plans have agents request
  dispatch from alarm or camera-verified events; Arlo Secure Plus includes emergency response;
  Google Home Premium Advanced includes emergency calling. Verified video changes police
  response priority (inferred).
- **Camera-to-camera handoff.** eufy's cross-camera tracking follows a person around the
  property and auto-edits one clip — users treat multiple cameras as one perimeter rather than
  separate feeds.
- **Continuous vs event recording tradeoffs.** 24/7 history needs wired power and either local
  disks (UniFi, Lorex, eufy Floodlight E340) or premium cloud (Nest: 10-day 24/7, wired cams
  only); battery cams are event-only almost everywhere except Reolink's Altas. Users mix: a
  wired doorbell + NVR for continuity, battery cams for corners (usage pattern inferred).
- **Offline behavior when Wi-Fi drops.** Cloud-only cams go blind except Nest's newer devices
  (1 hour of local event buffer, auto-upload on reconnect); hub/NVR systems keep recording
  locally; Aqara hub-cameras keep running local automations; SimpliSafe alarms keep working
  over cellular with 24h battery. This is a primary purchase differentiator for storm-prone
  areas (inferred).
- **Battery-life management.** Blink's two-year claim requires lithium AAs (alkaline halves
  to months; lithium works to -40°F); Ring sells quick-release packs so charging doesn't mean
  unmounting, plus behind-the-doorbell solar; Tapo C425 claims 300 days; eufy uses solar to
  make "never charge" the default; radar/PIR sensitivity and event frequency dominate real
  battery life (last point inferred).
